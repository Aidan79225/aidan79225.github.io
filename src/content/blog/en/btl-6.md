## Seeing yourself through a journal

Last time we covered [[btl-5|self-blindness, the first obstacle to innovation]] — you can't change a thing you haven't seen. The author's fix is unexpectedly simple: **spend five minutes a day writing a journal**. And the most interesting part is that the act of starting trains and reveals several things at once.

### One: your first reaction on hearing it
When you hear the assignment "write a journal every day", **don't rush into doing it — notice your first reaction**. Resistance, it sounds like a chore? Or "I've been doing this for years"? That reaction is itself a mirror — it tells you what your attitude to self-observation actually is.

### Two: how you plan to write it
Then the mechanics: **what time of day**, fixed? **By hand, on a computer, or on your phone**? There's no correct answer here; the point is to find a way you can **genuinely keep up** — the method you can sustain is the good method.

### Three: what to write
And the content? Answer: **anything to do with you**. If you don't know where to start, follow a simple structure — **facts → feelings → lessons**: write down what happened, then how you felt, then force yourself to distil one small lesson out of it.

What makes journalling unusual is this: you **can't know in advance** what you'll learn today, but as long as you write, you're **almost guaranteed to learn something**. And reading your own entries back after a while tends to produce insight you didn't have at the time.

## Reflection
My first reaction to the assignment was "I've been doing this for years". Every day I write a three-line mood journal in a tool I built myself, [TheBlock6](https://block6.aidan.tw/) — only three lines, but I think it covers the core of what a journal is for. I use the same tool to plan the next day's schedule, and it also holds quarterly OKR planning and GTD-style project breakdown. As for the "learning journal" part, my plan is to write it as blog posts or series entries — like this one.

So for me the hard part was never "what to write, how to write it". It's **whether I actually carve out the time to write and reflect**. Right now I do it before bed; TheBlock6 works on a phone, so even on a day with no time, three minutes is enough to finish it.
